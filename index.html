WordPress Contact Form Says Submitted but No Enquiry Received

The visitor submits the form and sees a success or thank-you message. Your team cannot find the enquiry in email, spam, form entries, or CRM. This page traces the full enquiry path — not “form broken,” not “missing email only,” not “spam only.”

Wrong symptom? Broad form failure diagnosis: contact form not sending. UI says sent but notification mail never appears anywhere: says sent, no email. Mail exists but lands in junk: emails going to spam.

1. What the success / thank-you message actually proves

A success message or thank-you page usually means the browser completed a form request that the site accepted. It does not prove your business received the enquiry in inbox, CRM, or even the form plugin’s storage.

  • Inline “Thank you” / AJAX success often fires after the plugin accepts the POST — before mail or CRM finishes.
  • A redirect to a thank-you URL can be static marketing content with no dependency on a saved entry.
  • Some themes show a soft success even when a later notification step fails silently.
  • Treat success as: visitor path reached “accepted” — then verify each later stage separately.

2. Whether the form stores submissions locally

First ask: did WordPress keep a record of this enquiry at all?

  • Open the form plugin’s Entries / Submissions / Leads list (Gravity Forms, WPForms, Fluent Forms, Formidable, etc.).
  • Search for a unique test phrase and the approximate submit time (with timezone).
  • If there is no entry, the break is at or before storage (validation, JS, AJAX error, wrong form ID, cache serving a stale form).
  • If there is an entry but nobody saw a notification, the visitor success was real — the disappearance is downstream (mail, routing, CRM).
  • Plugins that do not store entries by default leave you without this checkpoint — note that as a diagnostic gap.

3. Notification configuration

Storage and “thank you” can both succeed while admin notification is off or mis-aimed.

  • Confirm an admin / staff notification is enabled — not only a visitor auto-reply.
  • Check To, CC, BCC, and conditional logic (“send only if field X = …” can skip silent emails).
  • After migrations, templates often still point at old staff addresses or placeholder tags.
  • Separate “user confirmation” from “business notification” — one working does not imply the other.
  • If you only care about missing email after success, also read says sent but no email.

4. Recipient routing

The enquiry may have been delivered somewhere your team is not looking.

  • Confirm the exact To address in the live notification template (typos, old domains, personal inboxes of leavers).
  • Check shared / group inboxes, aliases, and forwarding rules.
  • Dynamic recipients from form fields can route to empty or wrong addresses when a field is blank.
  • Role-based or department routing may send to a mailbox nobody monitors after a reorg.
  • Ask: “Where was the last known good recipient?” and compare to today’s config.

5. Spam, junk, and quarantine

Sometimes the enquiry did arrive — just not in the primary inbox view.

  • Search Spam / Junk and provider quarantine for the unique test phrase.
  • Google Workspace / Microsoft 365 quarantine can hold first-time form mail.
  • Filters that auto-archive or label “Forms” can hide messages from the default inbox.
  • If you confirm the message exists in spam/junk, switch to the focused emails going to spam guide.

6. SMTP and mail-log evidence

Use logs to separate “never sent” from “sent but not found by staff.”

  • Note whether the site uses PHP mail(), an SMTP plugin, or a transactional API.
  • Run the plugin’s test email to the same production recipient; record accept/reject.
  • Look for SMTP / mailer log lines matching the test timestamp and To address.
  • Host mail logs (if available) showing deferred, rejected, or relayed for that attempt.
  • A clean “accepted by SMTP” with no inbox/spam find points to recipient-side routing or a different To than you think.

7. CRM, webhook, and integration failures

Many sites no longer rely on email alone — the “enquiry” may be meant to land in a CRM or Zapier-style hop.

  • Confirm whether success is supposed to create a CRM lead, HubSpot/Salesforce record, Google Sheet row, or Slack alert.
  • Check webhook / integration logs for HTTP errors, auth expiry, or rate limits around the test time.
  • A thank-you page can show even when the outbound webhook failed after the form saved locally.
  • Duplicate suppressions or “already exists” CRM rules can silently drop what staff expect as a new enquiry.
  • If email works but CRM does not (or the reverse), treat that as a multi-stage path — not one “form broken” ticket.

8. Confirmation-page false confidence

Success UX is for the visitor. Your operations need independent proof.

  • Do not close a “form works” ticket based only on seeing the thank-you page in a browser.
  • Verify storage entry, notification log, and recipient/CRM presence for the same unique phrase.
  • Cached thank-you URLs or CDN pages can look “alive” while the live form endpoint is broken for some users.
  • Multi-step forms: earlier steps may show progress; only the final submit should create the enquiry — confirm which step fires storage/notification.

9. Controlled test submission

One disciplined test beats days of vague reports.

  • Use a unique phrase (e.g. ENQ-2026-10-03-A) in the message body.
  • Record date/time with timezone, page URL, device, browser, and whether thank-you appeared.
  • Immediately check: form entries → notification logs → inbox + spam → CRM/webhook.
  • Optionally submit a second test to a mailbox you control, without leaving it as the live production To.
  • Change one variable between tests (recipient, SMTP, plugin toggle) — then retest.

10. Timestamp and evidence to keep

A short evidence pack turns “we never get enquiries” into a fixable path map.

  • Unique phrase, submit timestamp (with timezone), form URL, form ID/name.
  • Screenshot or note of the success/thank-you UI.
  • Entry present? Y/N. Notification log line. SMTP accept/reject.
  • Inboxes / spam / quarantine checked; CRM/webhook result.
  • One change made between attempts.

12. Isolated fault vs multi-stage enquiry-path failure

A single wrong To address, one disabled notification, or one spam-folder miss is often an isolated fix. Treat it as a wider enquiry-path problem when several of these hold:

  • Thank-you shows, but storage, mail, and CRM disagree with each other.
  • Failures differ by device, form variant, or day.
  • SMTP, recipient routing, and an integration all need coordinated changes.
  • You cannot reproduce with one controlled test well enough to verify a single change.

Isolated, reproducible break at one stage → short fix territory. Multi-stage / inconsistent disappearance across the path → map and repair the path, not one random toggle. This guide does not claim any sequence will recover every missing enquiry.
